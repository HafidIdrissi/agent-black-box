# Community launch plan

Goal: build a useful project that can earn contributions from at least 20 external people over time. This is an aspiration, not a guaranteed result.

## Before inviting contributors

- Demonstrate a real workflow with the included synthetic session.
- Keep setup working with one command and without paid services.
- Keep beginner issues independent, scoped, and accompanied by acceptance checks.
- Distinguish ready tasks from research proposals and blocked work.
- Keep the CI matrix green and fix onboarding problems promptly.

## Suggested first month

| Period | Maintainer focus |
| --- | --- |
| Week 1 | Test the getting-started guide with a few volunteers; resolve setup friction |
| Week 2 | Publish a short demo and one concrete debugging story in relevant communities |
| Week 3 | Support a small first-contribution cohort; review PRs and split over-large tasks |
| Week 4 | Release improvements, recognize contributors with their consent, and prune stale proposals |

Suggested posts belong in places that welcome project demonstrations. No unsolicited bulk messaging, purchased engagement, fake accounts, or contribution quotas.

## Useful measures

Track first-time contributors, time to a helpful first response, completed beginner tasks, repeat usage, and returning contributors. Define “external contributor” before reporting the count; do not count maintainer aliases, bots, or accounts created to inflate it.

120 open issues are not 120 validated user needs. Some are investigations and proposals. Close or revise low-value work as feedback arrives. A smaller active shortlist can coexist with a larger transparent backlog.
